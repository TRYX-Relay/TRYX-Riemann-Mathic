I-A. ONE-DIMENSIONAL LOCATION LAW
Let A be the admitted address domain, N:A→N_0 its numerical readout, and Loc:A→L a total single-valued location map. L is one affine line with an injective coordinate chart χ:L→R. Define X=χ∘Loc.
There is no independent Y component in this location type. Parent identity, number payload, perspective identity and retained history are record fields, not extra spatial coordinates.
THEOREM — X EXHAUSTS ADMITTED LOCATION.
Every admitted address has exactly one X location. No admitted location lies outside L, and distinct locations have distinct X coordinates.
Proof. Totality and single-valuedness of Loc give one element of L for every address. Composition with χ gives one coordinate. Injectivity of χ distinguishes any two different locations. QED.
This is a theorem about the explicitly typed model. It does not prove the type declaration for an independently specified external system. If N(A)=N_0, every natural-number value has at least one addressed representation on L. Uniqueness of location per numerical value additionally requires N(a)=N(b)⇒X(a)=X(b); uniqueness per address does not imply this.
Multiple addresses may occupy one location without becoming the same record. Recovery must use the complete retained record; coordinate X alone cannot recover different prime identities all placed at one crease.

I-B. EXHAUSTIVE CREASE PARTITION ON X
For a fixed identified parent K with endpoints a<b on L, let k=(χ(a)+χ(b))/2. Every admitted address belongs to exactly one class:
 X<k; X=k; X>k.
Proof. Trichotomy of real numbers. Grouping the first and third gives off-crease versus on-crease. There is no fourth spatial alternative and no independent Y destination in this model. QED.
One-dimensionality alone does not eliminate either off-crease class. For the surviving prime family, the declared matched-progress rule below eliminates both by forcing X=k.

I-C. SHARED LINE VERSUS LOCAL NORMALIZATION
The common-line coordinate X and the parent-normalized coordinate ξ_K=(X−χ(a))/(χ(b)−χ(a)) are distinct readouts. A matched event has ξ_K=1/2 and X=k.
All prime records share one literal X location only when their identified parents have the same midpoint in the common chart. Otherwise they share the normalized value 1/2 while retaining different common-line locations. A single global parent K is sufficient to establish the common midpoint; numerical equality of reference labels alone is not.
No independent Y is introduced by either readout. Neither readout is automatically the real part of a classical zeta input.

II. RELATIVE REFERENCES AND PARENT
Each surviving address p retains its own identified parent K_p, represented by endpoints a_p<b_p and L_p=b_p-a_p>0. The endpoints are parent positions, not the claim that both zero-valued reference roles occupy different positions in one frame.

Thus t_p+u_p=1. These are two perspectives on the same location and parent; their source identities do not merge. If the perspectives begin in separate charts, an admitted relative-coordinate map must first identify that common location and parent. No active reflection is required.

IV-A. PRIME-ON-CREASE LAWS AND PROOFS

PC1 — PRIME SURVIVOR LAW
p is an admitted numerical survivor iff p is prime.
Proof. Section I removes exactly 0, 1 and integers with a proper factorization uv, u,v>=2. Their complement in N_0 is exactly the primes. QED.

PC2 — MATCHED PRIME CREASE LAW
Every survivor with a supplied nondegenerate parent and the declared matching rule has its unique event at that parent's crease: ξ_p=1/2.
Proof. Incidence gives t_p+u_p=1 and matching gives t_p=u_p. Therefore both equal 1/2, giving the midpoint. Conversely the midpoint event satisfies both equations. QED.

PC3 — NO OFF-CREASE ADMITTED PRIME EVENT
